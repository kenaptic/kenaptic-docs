# Discover

The **Discover** group covers what content you have and how it connects. These pages fill in as
soon as your first source is connected.

## Overview

The **Overview** dashboard summarises the state of your estate:

- How much of your content is connected and cross-linked.
- What has changed since your last visit.
- What needs attention: a growing review queue, a source with no recent activity, a
  contradiction to reconcile.
- Estate health trends over time.

## Domains

**Domains** lists your connected sources. Each row is one source — a docs site, knowledge base,
blog, or forum — with its role (an estate you own, or a third-party link destination) and its
current status.

From here you can:

- **Add** a new source.
- Review how each source is being treated.
- See which sources Kenaptic can write to and which it only links to.

See [Connect a source](../getting-started/connect-a-source.md) for the details of adding one.

## Documents

**Documents** is the searchable list of every page Kenaptic has crawled across all your sources.
Use it to:

- Search and filter by source to find a specific page.
- Check coverage: confirm the pages you expected Kenaptic to read are present.
- Spot orphans and siloed pages. The Overview splits your estate three ways: *orphaned* (nothing
  links here at all), *siloed* (linked, but only from inside its own bucket) and *cross-linked*
  (already reachable from elsewhere). The fix differs by category: an orphan needs a link written
  into it, while a siloed page needs one from a different bucket. Siloed is normally the biggest
  group and the one cross-links address.

    These counts read your own links as well as Kenaptic's, so they describe your estate rather
    than Kenaptic's coverage of it. Until a crawl has captured them, the Overview shows
    *not measured* instead of a number.

## Knowledge Graph

The **Knowledge Graph** renders your estate as a graph: pages as points, relationships between
them as connections. Dense clusters and sparse, isolated pages are both visible. Use it to locate
strong hubs, find silos with no links between them, and track how the graph fills in as you
approve more links.
