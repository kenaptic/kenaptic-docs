# Discover your estate

The **Discover** group is where you understand what content you have and how it connects. These
pages fill in as soon as your first source is connected.

## Overview

Your daily starting point. The **Overview** dashboard summarises the health of your estate:

- How much of your content is connected and cross-linked.
- What's new since you last looked.
- What needs your attention — a review queue building up, a source that's gone quiet, a
  contradiction to reconcile.
- A sense of your progress over time, so you can see the estate getting healthier.

Think of it as the pulse of your content. If you only open one page a day, make it this one.

## Domains

**Domains** is where your sources live. Each row is one connected source — a docs site, knowledge
base, blog, or forum — with its role (an estate you own, or a third-party link destination) and its
current status.

From here you can:

- **Add** a new source.
- **Review** how each source is being treated.
- See at a glance which sources Kenaptic can write to and which it only links to.

See [Connect a source](../getting-started/connect-a-source.md) for the details of adding one.

## Documents

**Documents** is the full, searchable list of every page Kenaptic knows about across all your
sources. Use it to:

- **Search and filter** by source to find a specific page.
- **Check coverage** — confirm the pages you expected Kenaptic to read are actually there.
- **Spot orphans and siloed pages** — the Overview splits your estate three ways: *orphaned*
  (nothing links here at all), *siloed* (linked, but only from inside its own bucket) and
  *cross-linked* (already reachable from elsewhere). The distinction matters because the fix
  differs: an orphan needs a link written *into* it, while a siloed page needs one from a
  different bucket. Siloed is normally the biggest group and the one cross-links address.

    These counts read your own links as well as Kenaptic's, so they describe your estate rather
    than our coverage of it. Until a crawl has captured them the Overview says *not measured*
    rather than showing a number it cannot stand behind.

## Knowledge Graph

The **Knowledge Graph** turns your estate into a picture. Pages appear as points; the meaningful
relationships between them appear as connections. Clusters of well-connected content stand out — and
so do the sparse, isolated corners that need attention.

It's the fastest way to *see* the shape of your content: where the strong hubs are, where two silos
that should be linked aren't, and how much richer the map becomes as you approve more links.
